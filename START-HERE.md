# Start Here

This is the entry point for founders, engineers, product managers, designers, horticulture experts, operators, contractors, and AI agents.

## First 30 minutes

Read these files in order:

1. [`README.md`](README.md)
2. [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md)
3. [`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md)
4. [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md)
5. [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md)
6. [`OPEN-DECISIONS.md`](OPEN-DECISIONS.md)

Then read the documents relevant to your workstream.

## Three classes of knowledge

### Constitutional

Durable principles and system-wide rules. These change slowly and require explicit review.

### Specification

Approved definitions of domains, workflows, interfaces, data models, and expected behavior.

### Working

Research, proposals, unresolved questions, and experiments. Working material is not an approved product decision.

## Document statuses

- `proposed`: suggested but not yet accepted
- `draft`: actively being developed
- `in-review`: ready for designated reviewers
- `approved`: authoritative within its scope
- `deprecated`: still present but should not guide new work
- `archived`: retained only for history

## Before starting work

Confirm:

- the work appears in the roadmap or an approved issue;
- the relevant decision records and specifications have been read;
- an existing concept is not being duplicated;
- the document or code owner is clear;
- any unresolved dependency is recorded in `OPEN-DECISIONS.md`.

## Before finishing work

Update the relevant specification, decision record, status, roadmap, and changelog. Work is not complete when only the implementation changes.

## For AI agents

Do not treat drafts as authoritative. Cite the governing document or decision in proposed work. Flag contradictions rather than silently choosing one interpretation. Preserve established terminology and avoid inventing parallel concepts.
